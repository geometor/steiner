About¶

Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge & Repurposing It for Exact Symbolic Reasoning

About This Edition¶

The mission of this repository is to establish the definitive Digital Scriptorium edition of Jakob Steiner’s seminal 1833 treatise, Die geometrischen Constructionen, ausgeführt mittelst der geraden Linie und Eines festen Kreises, paired with exact symbolic geometry in geometor.steiner.

Euclidean geometry traditionally postulates both the ungraduated straightedge (Postulates 1–2) and the compass (Postulate 3). Between 1672 and 1890, synthetic and projective geometers discovered that either instrument can dispense with the other—provided a subtle asymmetry between circles and lines is respected:

  1. Compass Alone — Georg Mohr (1672) & Lorenzo Mascheroni (1797): First proved by Georg Mohr in Euclides Danicus (mohr-1672) and independently developed by Lorenzo Mascheroni in La Geometria del Compasso (mascheroni-1797), every Euclidean point-determination problem can be solved using only the compass. Straight lines cannot be continuously drawn, but any line is completely determined by two points \((A, B)\).

  2. Straightedge Alone with One Fixed Circle — Jean-Victor Poncelet (1822) & Jakob Steiner (1833): Conversely, a straightedge alone in an empty plane can execute only projectively invariant constructions (such as harmonic conjugates and polars) and cannot solve quadratic metric problems. Jean-Victor Poncelet (poncelet-1822) observed, and Jakob Steiner rigorously demonstrated in Die geometrischen Constructionen (steiner-1833), that a single permanently drawn circle together with its center \(M\) (fester Hülfskreis) suffices to execute every ruler-and-compass construction using the straightedge alone via eight canonical problems (§ 18, [68]–[85]) built from complete quadrangles, centers of similitude (Aehnlichkeitspunkte), and radical axes (Linie der gleichen Potenzen).

The 4-Track Codex Architecture¶

Following the GEOMETOR Digital Scriptorium canon, each section of Die geometrischen Constructionen is presented across four synchronized companion tracks:

  • German Primary Witness (``de``): Verbatim archival transcription based on the 1833 Berlin editio princeps (Ferdinand Dümmler) and Arthur Joachim von Oettingen’s 1895 Ostwald Klassiker Nr. 60 edition, preserving original pagination in square brackets [1]–[85].

  • English Translation (``en``): Direct scholarly translation into English, integrating modern projective formulations and Lemoine’s 1902 geometrographic analysis.

  • Analytical Summary (``summary``): Concise operational breakdown of theorems, projective configurations, operation symbols \(l_1 R_1 + l_2 R_2\), and complexity.

  • Commentary & Notes (``notes``): Historical lineage, projective foundations, Hilbert Chapter VII field hierarchies (Pythagorean vs. Euclidean quadratic closures), and why center \(M\) cannot be omitted.

The GEOMETOR Mission¶

The GEOMETOR project, directed by phi ARCHITECT, is dedicated to the systematic recovery, exact formalization, and dynamic re-expression of foundational geometric and philosophical knowledge:

  • Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge: Recovering the deductive structure of classical geometric, axiomatic, and philosophical treatises—elevating verbatim primary prose, archival scans, and calibrated .euclid-graphic diagrams into addressable semantic graphs.

  • Exact Symbolic Grounding (``geometor.model``): Anchoring geometric constructions in 100% exact symbolic algebra (SymPy) over closed-form radical fields without numerical approximations or floats.

  • Repurposing Knowledge Across Mediums: Deploying extracted structures across multi-track Book Codices, interactive SVG laboratories, and computational notebooks.
