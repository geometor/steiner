.. rubric:: The Role of Involutions & Orthogonal Pencils

1. Thales' Theorem as an Orthogonal Involution
----------------------------------------------

The brilliance of Steiner's Third Problem is its utilization of Thales' theorem: any angle inscribed in a semicircle of the fixed circle :math:`\Omega` is an exact right angle. Projectively, this establishes an elliptic involution of perpendicular rays centered at :math:`M`. By using the orthocenter :math:`H` of triangle :math:`\triangle abS`, the straightedge extracts right angles without computing lengths, relying entirely on the incidence of altitudes and diameters.

2. Geometric Economy in Lemoine's Géométrographie
-------------------------------------------------

In *Scientia* No. 18 (:cite:`lemoine-1902`, pp. 74–77), :person:`Émile Lemoine <lemoine>` singled out Steiner's parallel (:math:`S = 17`) and perpendicular (:math:`S = 15`) constructions as triumphs of projective economy. While modern drafting uses set-squares (:term:`équerre <equerre>`), Steiner demonstrated that the mathematical postulate of the compass is unnecessary for linear and angular affine operations once a single circle is present.
