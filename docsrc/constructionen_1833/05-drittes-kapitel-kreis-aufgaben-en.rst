Sixth Problem (``[76]``–``[78]``): Transferring a Given Segment
---------------------------------------------------------------

« *From a given point along a given ray, to lay off a segment equal in length to a given segment.* »

Given a segment :math:`M_1 a_1`, we regard it as the radius of an un-drawn (virtual) circle :math:`(M_1, r_1)`. To lay off this exact length from a new point :math:`M_2` along any ray:

1. Through the center :math:`M` of the permanently drawn fixed circle :math:`\Omega = (M, R)`, draw radius :math:`Ma` parallel to :math:`M_1 a_1` (by Problem 1).
2. The line :math:`aa_1` intersects the line of centers :math:`MM_1` in the external :term:`center of similitude <aehnlichkeitspunkte>` :math:`A`, while opposite radius :math:`Ma'` yields the internal center :math:`I`.
3. Through centers :math:`A` and :math:`I`, the homothetic ratio :math:`r_1 : R` is established. Drawing rays through :math:`A` or :math:`I` transfers length :math:`M_1 a_1` to any destination origin :math:`M_2` in any direction using straightedge alone!

Seventh Problem (``[78]``–``[82]``): Intersecting a Straight Line with an Un-Drawn Circle
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

« *To find the mutual intersection points of a given straight line and a given (un-drawn, virtual) circle :math:`(M_1, r_1)`.* »

Let :math:`G_1` be the given line, and :math:`(M_1, r_1)` the un-drawn circle defined by its center and radius segment.

1. Construct the center of similitude :math:`A` of fixed circle :math:`(M, R)` and virtual circle :math:`(M_1, r_1)` (by Problem 6).
2. Homothetically project line :math:`G_1` from center :math:`A` onto a corresponding line :math:`G` relative to the **actually drawn fixed circle** :math:`(M, R)`: line :math:`G \parallel G_1`.
3. Line :math:`G` intersects the drawn circumference of :math:`\Omega` in two visible points :math:`g` and :math:`h`.
4. Connect :math:`Ag` and :math:`Ah`: their intersections with the given line :math:`G_1` are the exact intersection points :math:`g_1, h_1` of the un-drawn circle with the given line!

Eighth Problem (``[82]``–``[85]``): Intersecting Two Un-Drawn Circles
---------------------------------------------------------------------

« *To find the mutual intersection points of two given (un-drawn, virtual) circles :math:`(M_1, r_1)` and :math:`(M_2, r_2)`.* »

Neither circle is drawn on the paper; each is specified only by its center point and a radius segment.

1. Using the straightedge alone (Chapter II, § 17), construct their :term:`line of equal powers <linie-der-gleichen-potenzen>` (radical axis).
2. Because the radical axis is the straight carrier line containing the common intersection points (if they are real), the problem of intersecting two circles is immediately reduced to finding the intersection of a straight line (the radical axis) with one of the circles (say :math:`M_1`).
3. But this is precisely the **Seventh Problem**!

This concludes the constructive proof that **every Euclidean ruler-and-compass construction can be executed with the straightedge alone given a single fixed circle and its center :math:`M`**.

Concluding Remark: The Indispensability of the Center :math:`M`
---------------------------------------------------------------

If only the continuous circle circumference :math:`\Omega` were drawn **without** its center :math:`M`, finding :math:`M` by straightedge alone is mathematically impossible. Any projective collineation of the plane preserving :math:`\Omega` (an automorphic collineation isomorphic to the hyperbolic isometry group of the disk) preserves all straight lines and incidences, yet moves the Euclidean center :math:`M` to other interior points. Only the presence of :math:`M` fixes the Euclidean metric involution and enables complete geometric constructibility.
