========================================================================================================================
Notes & Commentary: Algebraic Field Closures & Hilbert's Chapter VII
========================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: The Exact Algebraic Boundary Between Eichmaß and Traced Circle

1. Hilbert's Grundlagen Chapter VII (§§ 36–39)
-----------------------------------------------

In 1899, :person:`David Hilbert <hilbert>` published *Grundlagen der Geometrie* (:cite:`hilbert-1899`), culminating in Chapter VII: *Die geometrischen Konstruktionen auf Grund der Axiome I–IV*. Hilbert analyzed what can be constructed using only a straightedge and an *Eichmaß* (:term:`Eichmaß <eichmass>`, an instrument laying off a unit segment :math:`1` along any line, equivalent to a pair of dividers fixed at radius :math:`1`):

* Hilbert proved that a point is constructible with straightedge and *Eichmaß* if and only if its coordinates lie in the **Pythagorean field closure** :math:`\Omega_{\text{pyth}}` generated by operations :math:`\sqrt{1 + \omega^2}`.
* In particular, the square root of an arbitrary positive element :math:`\sqrt{\alpha}` cannot generally be extracted: :math:`\sqrt{1 + \sqrt{2}} \notin \Omega_{\text{pyth}}`! Therefore, straightedge and *Eichmaß* can construct only a strictly smaller subfield of Euclidean geometry.

2. The Continuous Curve as an Algebraic Oracle
----------------------------------------------

By contrast, in **Poncelet–Steiner (1822 / 1833)**, the presence of **one permanently traced circle locus** :math:`\Omega` bridges this gap. Because any secant physically intersects the circumference of :math:`\Omega`, the actual intersection point provides the square root of arbitrary positive quadratic polynomials :math:`\sqrt{\alpha}` via homothety!

Thus, a single drawn circle upgrades straightedge constructibility from the Pythagorean field to the **full Euclidean quadratic closure** :math:`\mathbb{E}`.

3. The Dual Symmetry with Mascheroni
------------------------------------

The duality between Mascheroni (1797) and Steiner (1833) is now algebraically and visually complete:

* **Mascheroni**: Eliminates the straightedge (:math:`l_1 = l_2 = 0`) by representing straight lines as point-pairs :math:`(A, B)` and using circle inversions (:person:`Adler <adler>`, :cite:`adler-1890`).
* **Steiner**: Eliminates the compass (:math:`m_1 = m_2 = m_3 = 0`) once a single fixed circle :math:`(M, R)` is drawn, representing circles as center and radius-point :math:`(M_1, a_1)` and using homothety and radical axes.
