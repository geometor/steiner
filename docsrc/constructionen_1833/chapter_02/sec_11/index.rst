§ 11: The Center of Similitude of Two Figures
=============================================

.. rubric:: Jakob Steiner — *Die geometrischen Constructionen* (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

**Chapter II — Part II: Centers of Similitude.**

.. picture:: 0033.g1.svg
   :alt: Figure 13 — Center of similitude A mapping triangle abc to a1 b1 c1 (§ 11)
   :caption: Fig. 13 — Center of similitude A pairing corresponding points a, a1; b, b1; c, c1 in constant ratio n : n1 (§ 11)

.. collapse:: Archival Plate — Folio 0033, Fig. 13 (1895, p. 29)

   .. picture:: 0033.g1.png
      :alt: Archival scan of Fig. 13 from Ostwald's Klassiker Nr. 60 (p. 29)

Drawing rays in all directions through a fixed point :g:`A <0033.g1:A>`, let every point :g:`a <0033.g1:a>` of plane :math:`E` correspond to a point :g:`a_1 <0033.g1:a1>` of plane :math:`E_1` on the same ray :g:`Aa_1 <0033.g1:/ A a1 />` such that all distance pairs :math:`Aa : Aa_1` maintain a constant ratio :math:`n : n_1`.

Six fundamental laws follow (I–VI):

* **I.** Any line :g:`ab <0033.g1:/ a b />` corresponds to a **parallel** line :g:`a_1 b_1 <0033.g1:/ a1 b1 />` in the ratio :math:`ab : a_1 b_1 = n : n_1`.
* **II.** Every line passing through :g:`A` corresponds to itself.
* **III–IV.** Intersections correspond to intersections, and parallel lines correspond to parallel lines.
* **V–VI.** Any polygon :g:`abc <0033.g1:< a b c >>` or circle corresponds to a similar and similarly situated polygon :g:`a_1 b_1 c_1 <0033.g1:< a1 b1 c1 >>` or circle. Two figures have an *external* center of similitude :math:`A` when corresponding points lie on the same side of the center, and an *internal* center :math:`I` when they lie on opposite sides.
