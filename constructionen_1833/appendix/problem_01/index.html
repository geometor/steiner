Appendix Problem 1: Polygon Circumscribed About One and Inscribed in Another¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

« Given any two triangles in a plane, to find a third triangle that is simultaneously circumscribed about the first and inscribed in the second. »

Let \(B, B_1, B_2\) be the vertices of the first triangle and \(A, A_1, A_2\) the extended sides of the second. Starting from three trial points \(\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}, \mathfrak{c}\) on \(A\), trace rays cyclically through \(B \to A_1 \to B_1 \to A_2 \to B_2 \to A\) to obtain image points \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) on \(A\). Projecting the three pairs onto fixed circle \(\Omega\) from pole \(P\) gives axis \(\mathfrak{pq}\), whose intersections \(r, s\) with \(\Omega\) yield up to two self-closing triangles (or none if \(\mathfrak{pq}\) misses \(\Omega\)).

Remark (p. 68): The exact same method extends to arbitrary \(n\)-gons (quadrilaterals, pentagons, etc.).
