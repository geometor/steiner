Appendix Problem 22: Plotting a Circle in the Field Around an Inaccessible Center¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

« In the field, when the center \(M_1\) and one point \(a_1\) on the circumference of a circle are given, yet an obstacle \(RS\) separates them so that the radius cannot be laid off continuously across the terrain, to determine arbitrarily many other points \(c_1, d_1, \dots\) of that circle using only surveying staves and a measuring chain. »

▸ Archival Plate — Folio 0082, Fig. 25 (1895, p. 78)

  1. Extend the visible segment beyond a1 by aligning two equal chain lengths a1 m \(=\) mn (pp. 78–80 / [108]–[110]). On clear ground around a point M, sweep a fixed auxiliary circle M using the chain as radius, with diameter ab parallel to M1 a1.

  2. Mark external and internal similitude stakes \(A\) (meeting a1 a) and \(I\) (meeting a1 b) along the line of centers M1 M.

  3. For any diameter \(cd\) traced by the chain around M, the sightlines \(Ac \cap Id\) and \(Ad \cap Ic\) give exact target points c1, d1 on the blocked circle! (Pp. 79–80 extend the method even if three circumcircle points \(a_1, d_1, e_1\) replace \(M_1, a_1\).)
