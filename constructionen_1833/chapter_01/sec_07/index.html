§ 7: Parallels from Rational Segment Ratios¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

▸ Archival Plate — Folio 0019, Fig. 6 (1895, p. 15)

Problem (§ 7): Given two adjacent collinear segments BD and DC having any given rational ratio \(a : b\), to draw a line parallel to BC through any given point using the straightedge alone.

Let \(a > b\) be coprime integers. Constructing the fourth harmonic point E conjugate to C with respect to B and D (§ 5, I) produces segment \(CE = x\) satisfying \(a : b = (a + b + x) : x\), whence \(x = \frac{b(a+b)}{a-b}\), and therefore:

\[BC : CE = a - b : b \quad (\text{or } a : z = a - b : 2b \text{ with } z = DE).\]

Iterating this Euclidean subtraction step (\(a, b \to a-b, b \to \dots\)) strictly decreases the integer terms until two equal adjacent segments in the ratio \(1 : 1\) are obtained—reducing the construction immediately to § 6, I!
