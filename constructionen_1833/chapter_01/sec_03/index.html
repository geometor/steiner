§ 3: Harmonic Rays and Harmonic Point Ranges¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

▸ Archival Plate — Folio 0010, Fig. 1 (1895, p. 6)

  1. Let ABC be any triangle: from vertex B, let ray Bb pass through the midpoint b of base AC, and let ray Bd be parallel to base AC. If any transversal straight line ad is drawn through midpoint b, cutting the sides BABC and rays BbBd in four points a, b, c, d, then by similar triangles \(\triangle aAb \sim \triangle aBd\) and \(\triangle cCb \sim \triangle cBd\), since Ab = Cb:

\[\frac{ab}{ad} = \frac{cb}{cd} \quad \text{or} \quad ab : bc = ad : cd.\]

Four concurrent straight lines \(a, b, c, d\) emanating from a common vertex B form a harmonic pencil of rays if every transversal intersecting them in four points \(A, B, C, D\) produces a harmonic range, defined by the invariant cross-ratio:

\[(A, B; C, D) = \frac{AC}{BC} : \frac{AD}{BD} = -1.\]

II–IV. Every transversal cutting four harmonic rays cuts them in four harmonic points; conversely, four rays joining any point to four harmonic points form a harmonic pencil. When \(D\) recedes to infinity (\(Bd \parallel AC\)), \(C\) is the exact midpoint of AB. Moreover, if two conjugate harmonic rays \(b, d\) are perpendicular to each other, they bisect the interior and supplementary exterior angles between \(a\) and \(c\).
