§ 9: Perpendiculars and Angle Operations from a Given Square¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

Part II.C — When a Square Is Given.

▸ Archival Plate — Folio 0025, Fig. 11 (1895, p. 21)

Besides all affine constructions obtainable from a parallelogram (§ 8, I), when the parallelogram is specifically a square ABCD with center E = AC \cap BD, one can also with the straightedge alone:

  1. Drop a perpendicular onto any given line gf from any given point i: draw GEF through E parallel to gf; draw FH parallel to BC meeting AB at H, then HI parallel to diagonal AC meeting BC at I. Since \(\triangle EBI \cong \triangle ECF\), \(\angle IEF = \angle BEC = 90^\circ\), so a parallel to IEK through i is the required perpendicular!

  2. Bisect any given right angle (via isosceles right triangle \(\triangle IEF\) and perpendicular EL to \(IF\)).

  3. Multiply any given angle by any integer \(n\) (repeatedly applying Problem (a) and the harmonic angle-doubling property of § 5, III).
