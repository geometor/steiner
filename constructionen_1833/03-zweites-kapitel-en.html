Chapter II: Centers of Similitude and Radical Axes of Circles (§§ 11–17)¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen, ausgeführt mittelst der geraden Linie und Eines festen Kreises (Berlin, 1833, pp. 29–50 / [29]–[67])

In This Chapter

  • §§ 11–13. Centers and Axes of Similitude of Circles

  • §§ 14–17. The Power of a Point and the Line of Equal Powers (Radical Axis)

§§ 11–13. Centers and Axes of Similitude of Circles¶

Given two coplanar circles \(M\) and \(M_1\) with radii \(R\) and \(r_1\), the pairs of direct and transverse common tangents intersect at two points on the line of centers \(MM_1\): the external center of similitude A and the internal center of similitude I.

These points divide the segment of centers \(MM_1\) harmonically in the exact ratio of the radii:

\[\frac{\overrightarrow{AM}}{\overrightarrow{AM_1}} = \frac{R}{r_1}, \qquad \frac{\overrightarrow{IM}}{\overrightarrow{IM_1}} = -\frac{R}{r_1}.\]

Any ray drawn through \(A\) or \(I\) establishes a central dilation (homothety) mapping the circumference of circle \(M\) directly onto the circumference of circle \(M_1\).

Theorem (§ 13): Given three coplanar circles, their six mutual centers of similitude lie three by three along four axes of similitude (Aehnlichkeitsaxen):

  1. One external axis of similitude, passing through the three external centers \((A_{12}, A_{23}, A_{31})\).

  2. Three mixed axes of similitude, each containing one external and two internal centers (e.g. \(A_{12}, I_{23}, I_{31}\)).

Because parallel radii can be drawn with the straightedge once a fixed circle is present, all six centers and four axes of similitude are constructible without drawing any compass arcs!

§§ 14–17. The Power of a Point and the Line of Equal Powers (Radical Axis)¶

If an arbitrary secant line through a point \(P\) intersects a circle \((M, R)\) at points \(A\) and \(B\), the product of the signed distances:

\[\text{Pow}_M(P) = \overrightarrow{PA} \cdot \overrightarrow{PB} = d^2 - R^2 \qquad (d = PM)\]

is constant for all secants. Steiner designated this quantity \(d^2 - R^2\) the power of the point with respect to the circle.

For two given circles \((M_1, r_1)\) and \((M_2, r_2)\), the locus of all points \(P\) having equal powers:

\[d_1^2 - r_1^2 = d_2^2 - r_2^2\]

is a straight line perpendicular to the line of centers \(M_1 M_2\), called the line of equal powers (or radical axis / axe radicale).

If the two circles intersect in two real points, their radical axis is simply their common secant chord. If they do not intersect, their radical axis can be constructed with a straightedge alone (§ 17) by introducing an auxiliary circle that cuts both, finding the two chords of intersection, and dropping the perpendicular from their meeting point!

For three circles with non-collinear centers, the three radical axes meet at a single unique point: the radical center (Punkt der gleichen Potenzen).
