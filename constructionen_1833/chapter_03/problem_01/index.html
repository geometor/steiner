First Problem (§ 18, [68]–[71]): Drawing a Parallel Through Any Given Point¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

« Through any given point, to draw a straight line parallel to any given straight line. »

▸ Archival Plate — Folio 0055, Fig. 19 (1895, p. 51)
Case a: When the given line passes through the center :math:`M` of the fixed circle.

The given line cuts the fixed circle \(\Omega\) in a diameter ab bisected at M. Using the complete quadrangle construction of Chapter I (§ 6), any external point is joined with \(a, M, b\) to yield the exact fourth harmonic parallel at infinity in 6 straight lines:

\[\mathrm{Op.}_{\text{Parallel}} : 11R_1 + 6R_2, \qquad S = 17, \quad E = 11, \quad N_{\text{circles}} = 0.\]
Case b: When the given line :g:`ef <0055.g1:/ e f />` does not pass through :math:`M`.

As illustrated in Fig. 19, draw any diameter ab meeting ef at g, pick any point c on \(\Omega\), and construct the symmetrical chords cd and c1 d1 via the intersections through M, transferring the given direction to a parallel diameter through \(M\), which reduces the construction through any external point to Case a along hi.
