§ 18: Introduction — Reduction of All Constructions to Eight Fundamental Problems¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

Solution of all geometric problems by means of the straightedge, when any single fixed circle is given.

Having established the harmonic properties of rectilinear figures in Chapter I and the centers of similitude and radical axes of circles in Chapter II, we now fulfill the promise of the Introductory Survey (§ 1): solving the eight fundamental problems solely with the ungraduated straightedge in the presence of a single fixed auxiliary circle \(\Omega\) with given center \(M\).

Every Euclidean ruler-and-compass construction reduces to a finite sequence of three elementary intersection operations:

  1. Line–Line Meet: Directly traced with the straightedge.

  2. Line–Circle Meet: Solved for any un-drawn circle \((M_1, r_1)\) by Problem 7 via homothetic projection onto \(\Omega\).

  3. Circle–Circle Meet: Solved for any two un-drawn circles by Problem 8 via their line of equal powers (reducing directly to Problem 7).

Because Problems 1–5 furnish parallels, rational segment divisions, perpendiculars, and angle transfers/bisections using \(\Omega\), and Problem 6 transfers segments via similitude centers, all of plane elementary geometry is reduced to pure straightedge incidences.
