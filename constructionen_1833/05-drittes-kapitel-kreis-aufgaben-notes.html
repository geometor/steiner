Notes & Commentary: Algebraic Field Closures & Hilbert’s Chapter VII¶

The Exact Algebraic Boundary Between Eichmaß and Traced Circle

1. Hilbert’s Grundlagen Chapter VII (§§ 36–39)¶

In 1899, David Hilbert published Grundlagen der Geometrie (hilbert-1899), culminating in Chapter VII: Die geometrischen Konstruktionen auf Grund der Axiome I–IV. Hilbert analyzed what can be constructed using only a straightedge and an Eichmaß (Eichmaß, an instrument laying off a unit segment \(1\) along any line, equivalent to a pair of dividers fixed at radius \(1\)):

  • Hilbert proved that a point is constructible with straightedge and Eichmaß if and only if its coordinates lie in the Pythagorean field closure \(\Omega_{\text{pyth}}\) generated by operations \(\sqrt{1 + \omega^2}\).

  • In particular, the square root of an arbitrary positive element \(\sqrt{\alpha}\) cannot generally be extracted: \(\sqrt{1 + \sqrt{2}} \notin \Omega_{\text{pyth}}\)! Therefore, straightedge and Eichmaß can construct only a strictly smaller subfield of Euclidean geometry.

2. The Continuous Curve as an Algebraic Oracle¶

By contrast, in Poncelet–Steiner (1822 / 1833), the presence of one permanently traced circle locus \(\Omega\) bridges this gap. Because any secant physically intersects the circumference of \(\Omega\), the actual intersection point provides the square root of arbitrary positive quadratic polynomials \(\sqrt{\alpha}\) via homothety!

Thus, a single drawn circle upgrades straightedge constructibility from the Pythagorean field to the full Euclidean quadratic closure \(\mathbb{E}\).

3. The Dual Symmetry with Mascheroni¶

The duality between Mascheroni (1797) and Steiner (1833) is now algebraically and visually complete:

  • Mascheroni: Eliminates the straightedge (\(l_1 = l_2 = 0\)) by representing straight lines as point-pairs \((A, B)\) and using circle inversions (Adler, adler-1890).

  • Steiner: Eliminates the compass (\(m_1 = m_2 = m_3 = 0\)) once a single fixed circle \((M, R)\) is drawn, representing circles as center and radius-point \((M_1, a_1)\) and using homothety and radical axes.
