Chapter I: Harmonic Rays and Points, Complete Quadrangle and Polars (§§ 3–10)¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen, ausgeführt mittelst der geraden Linie und Eines festen Kreises (Berlin, 1833, pp. 7–28 / [7]–[28])

In This Chapter

  • §§ 3–5. Harmonic Rays and Harmonic Point Ranges

  • § 6. The Complete Quadrangle and Complete Quadrilateral

  • §§ 7–10. Polar Lines and Poles with Respect to a Circle

§§ 3–5. Harmonic Rays and Harmonic Point Ranges¶

Four concurrent straight lines \(a, b, c, d\) emanating from a common vertex \(S\) form a harmonic pencil of rays if every transversal intersecting them in four points \(A, B, C, D\) produces a harmonic range, defined by the invariant cross-ratio:

\[(A, B; C, D) = \frac{AC}{BC} : \frac{AD}{BD} = -1.\]

Under this condition, points \(C\) and \(D\) harmonically separate the pair \(A, B\). In particular, if \(D\) recedes to infinity along the carrier line (\(AD/BD \to 1\)), then \(AC/BC = -1\), meaning that \(C\) is the exact midpoint of segment \(AB\)!

§ 6. The Complete Quadrangle and Complete Quadrilateral¶

Four coplanar points, no three collinear, constitute a complete quadrangle. They determine six connecting lines (sides) meeting pairwise in three diagonal points.

Dually, four coplanar straight lines, no three concurrent, constitute a complete quadrilateral.

Theorem: On every diagonal of a complete quadrangle, the two opposite sides and the remaining two diagonal points harmonically separate each other.

This yields the fundamental straightedge construction:

Given three collinear points :math:`A, B, C`, to construct the fourth harmonic conjugate :math:`D` using only a straightedge.

  1. Choose an arbitrary vertex \(S\) off the line \(AB\).

  2. Draw rays \(SA, SB, SC\).

  3. Draw an arbitrary transversal through \(A\) meeting \(SC\) at \(P\) and \(SB\) at \(Q\).

  4. Join \(BP\) and \(SQ\) meeting at \(R\).

  5. Join \(AR\): its intersection with line \(AB\) is the required fourth harmonic point \(D\)!

f. 0020 · p. 20

§§ 7–10. Polar Lines and Poles with Respect to a Circle¶

If any secant drawn through a pole \(P\) cuts a given circle in points \(A\) and \(B\), and on this secant the fourth harmonic point \(Q\) is determined such that \((A, B; P, Q) = -1\), the locus of all such conjugate points \(Q\) as the secant rotates is a single straight line: the polar \(p\) of \(P\) with respect to the circle.

Because the polar relation is purely harmonic, the straightedge alone constructs it for any point \(P\):

  1. Draw two secants through \(P\) cutting the circle at \(A, B\) and \(C, D\).

  2. Join the cross-chords: \(AD \cap BC = E\) and \(AC \cap BD = F\).

  3. Line \(EF\) is the polar line \(p\) of \(P\)!

If \(P\) lies outside the circle, polar \(p\) cuts the circumference in the exact points of contact of the two tangents from \(P\)—allowing tangents to any drawn circle to be constructed with a straightedge alone in \(16R_1 + 9R_2\) (\(S = 25\), zero circles)!
