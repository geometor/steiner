§ 10: Harmonic Properties, Poles, and Polars of the Circle¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

▸ Archival Plate — Folio 0027, Fig. 12 (1895, p. 23)

Chapter II — Part I: Harmonic Properties of the Circle.

  1. Let a, b, c, d be four harmonic points on a line, and let B be a point such that the conjugate rays Ba and Bc are perpendicular; by § 3.IV they bisect the angles between Bb and Bd, and the locus of B is the circle M on diameter ac.

II–IV. If any secant drawn through a fixed pole P cuts a given circle M in points a and c, and on this secant the fourth harmonic point b is determined such that \((a, c; b, P) = -1\), the locus of all such conjugate points \(b\) as the secant rotates is a single straight line: the polar pq of P with respect to the circle.

Because the polar relation is purely harmonic, the straightedge alone constructs it for any point \(P\):

  1. Draw two secants through \(P\) cutting the circle at \(A, B\) and \(C, D\).

  2. Join the cross-chords: \(AD \cap BC = E\) and \(AC \cap BD = F\).

  3. Line \(EF\) is the polar line pq of \(P\)!

If P lies outside the circle, its polar pq cuts the circumference in the exact points of contact p, q of the two tangents dp, dq from P—allowing tangents to any drawn circle to be constructed with a straightedge alone in \(16R_1 + 9R_2\) (\(S = 25\), zero circles)!
