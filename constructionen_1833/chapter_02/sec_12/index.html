§ 12: External and Internal Centers of Similitude of Two Circles¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

▸ Archival Plate — Folio 0036, Fig. 14 (1895, p. 32)

  1. Given any two coplanar circles M and M1 with radii \(R\) and \(r_1\), draw any two parallel diameters ab and a1 b1. The lines joining endpoints on the same side of the line of centers MM1 (aa1, bb1) meet the axis in the external center of similitude A, while cross-joins (ab1, ba1) meet the axis in the internal center of similitude I:

\[\frac{\overrightarrow{AM}}{\overrightarrow{AM_1}} = \frac{R}{r_1}, \qquad \frac{\overrightarrow{IM}}{\overrightarrow{IM_1}} = -\frac{R}{r_1} \implies (M, M_1; A, I) = -1.\]

II–III. Every ray through A or I establishes a direct homothety mapping \((M, R)\) onto \((M_1, r_1)\).

Footnote Application (Fig. 15, pp. 37–40): Applying § 11 to triangle abc and its medial triangle a1 b1 c1 (in the ratio \(2 : 1\) from their internal center of similitude I), it follows immediately that the circumcenter M, centroid I, orthocenter H, and nine-point center M1 lie on one straight line with \(HI = 2 IM\) and \(M_1\) bisecting HM.
