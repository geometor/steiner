§ 11: The Center of Similitude of Two Figures¶

Jakob Steiner — Die geometrischen Constructionen (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

Chapter II — Part II: Centers of Similitude.

▸ Archival Plate — Folio 0033, Fig. 13 (1895, p. 29)

Drawing rays in all directions through a fixed point A, let every point a of plane \(E\) correspond to a point a1 of plane \(E_1\) on the same ray Aa1 such that all distance pairs \(Aa : Aa_1\) maintain a constant ratio \(n : n_1\).

Six fundamental laws follow (I–VI):

  • I. Any line ab corresponds to a parallel line a1 b1 in the ratio \(ab : a_1 b_1 = n : n_1\).

  • II. Every line passing through A corresponds to itself.

  • III–IV. Intersections correspond to intersections, and parallel lines correspond to parallel lines.

  • V–VI. Any polygon abc or circle corresponds to a similar and similarly situated polygon a1 b1 c1 or circle. Two figures have an external center of similitude \(A\) when corresponding points lie on the same side of the center, and an internal center \(I\) when they lie on opposite sides.
