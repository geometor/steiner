Geometrical Constructions¶

Die geometrischen Constructionen, ausgeführt mittelst der geraden Linie und Eines festen Kreises, als Lehrgegenstand auf höheren Unterrichts-Anstalten und zur praktischen Benutzung — Jakob Steiner (Berlin, 1833)

Author: Jakob Steiner
First Edition (Berlin, 1833): Ferdinand Dümmler (110 S., 2 Kupfertafeln, Fig. 1–25).
Ostwald’s Klassiker Edition (Leipzig, 1895): Nr. 60, edited by A. J. von Oettingen (Wilhelm Engelmann).
English Translation: Scholarly edition curated for the GEOMETOR Digital Scriptorium.
Synchronized 4-Track Codex: English · German · Summary · Notes, across 51 atomic leaf pages paired with 26 calibrated archival plates and exact symbolic geometry in geometor.steiner.constructions.

“All geometric problems of elementary geometry can be solved using only the straightedge, provided that a single fixed auxiliary circle together with its center is drawn in the plane.”

—Jakob Steiner, Die geometrischen Constructionen, § 1 (p. 3)

Table of Contents¶

Introductory Survey: Reduction of Compass Operations to the Straightedge (§§ 1–2)

The Introductory Survey states the core thesis of the Poncelet–Steiner theorem: every construction of elementary Euclidean geometry can be executed with the straightedge alone, once a single fixed auxiliary circle with its center M is permanently drawn in the plane. Steiner outlines the eight fundamental problems solved in Chapter III and surveys the historical lineage through Mascheroni (1797), Poncelet (1822), Lambert (1759), and Brianchon (1817).

Chapter I: Harmonic Pencils, Complete Quadrangles, and Polars (§§ 3–10)

Chapter I establishes the pure straightedge toolkit from rectilinear figures alone: harmonic rays and harmonic ranges (§§ 3–5), complete quadrangle constructions when a parallel or bisected segment is given (§§ 6–7), arbitrary parallel division and multiplication (§ 8), polars and tangents with respect to circles and curves (§ 9, Fig. 11).

Chapter II: Pole–Polar Theory, Similitude Centers, and Radical Axes (§§ 10–17)

Chapter II investigates the circle under three major divisions: harmonic pole-polar properties of the circle constructed by straightedge alone (§ 10, Fig. 12); external and internal centers of similitude (Aehnlichkeitspunkte) and the four axes of similitude of three circles (§§ 11–13, Figs. 13–16); and Steiner’s theory of the power of a point, radical axes (Linien der gleichen Potenzen), radical centers, and common power (§§ 14–17, Figs. 17–18).

Chapter III: The Eight Fundamental Problems & Completeness (§§ 18–19)

Chapter III executes the constructive proof of the Poncelet–Steiner theorem across the eight fundamental problems (§ 18, Figs. 19–24): parallels, rational segment scaling, perpendiculars, angle copying and bisection, segment transfer via similitude, line–circle intersections via homothety onto the fixed circle, and circle–circle intersections via the radical axis—closing in § 19 with the proof that the center M itself is indispensable.

Appendix: Miscellaneous Conic & Surveying Constructions (§ 20 & Annotations)

The Appendix demonstrates the power of the fixed-circle straightedge method across 22 higher constructions: inscribed/circumscribed triangle loops (Problem 1), twenty paired projective-dual conic incidence, tangent, and Castillon–Poncelet polygon problems (Problems 2–21), and practical field surveying around terrain obstacles using staves and chain (Problem 22, Fig. 25), followed by A. J. von Oettingen’s 1895 editorial annotations.
