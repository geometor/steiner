§§ 11–13. Centers and Axes of Similitude of Circles
---------------------------------------------------

Given two coplanar circles :math:`M` and :math:`M_1` with radii :math:`R` and :math:`r_1`, the pairs of direct and transverse common tangents intersect at two points on the line of centers :math:`MM_1`: the **external center of similitude** :term:`A <aehnlichkeitspunkte>` and the **internal center of similitude** :term:`I <aehnlichkeitspunkte>`.

These points divide the segment of centers :math:`MM_1` harmonically in the exact ratio of the radii:

.. math::

   \frac{\overrightarrow{AM}}{\overrightarrow{AM_1}} = \frac{R}{r_1}, \qquad \frac{\overrightarrow{IM}}{\overrightarrow{IM_1}} = -\frac{R}{r_1}.

Any ray drawn through :math:`A` or :math:`I` establishes a central dilation (homothety) mapping the circumference of circle :math:`M` directly onto the circumference of circle :math:`M_1`.

*Theorem (§ 13)*: Given three coplanar circles, their six mutual centers of similitude lie three by three along **four axes of similitude** (:term:`Aehnlichkeitsaxen <aehnlichkeitsaxe>`):

1. One **external axis of similitude**, passing through the three external centers :math:`(A_{12}, A_{23}, A_{31})`.
2. Three **mixed axes of similitude**, each containing one external and two internal centers (e.g. :math:`A_{12}, I_{23}, I_{31}`).

Because parallel radii can be drawn with the straightedge once a fixed circle is present, all six centers and four axes of similitude are constructible without drawing any compass arcs!

§§ 14–17. The Power of a Point and the Line of Equal Powers (Radical Axis)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

If an arbitrary secant line through a point :math:`P` intersects a circle :math:`(M, R)` at points :math:`A` and :math:`B`, the product of the signed distances:

.. math::

   \text{Pow}_M(P) = \overrightarrow{PA} \cdot \overrightarrow{PB} = d^2 - R^2 \qquad (d = PM)

is constant for all secants. Steiner designated this quantity :math:`d^2 - R^2` the :term:`power of the point <potenz-eines-punktes>` with respect to the circle.

For two given circles :math:`(M_1, r_1)` and :math:`(M_2, r_2)`, the locus of all points :math:`P` having equal powers:

.. math::

   d_1^2 - r_1^2 = d_2^2 - r_2^2

is a straight line perpendicular to the line of centers :math:`M_1 M_2`, called the :term:`line of equal powers <linie-der-gleichen-potenzen>` (or *radical axis* / *axe radicale*).

If the two circles intersect in two real points, their radical axis is simply their common secant chord. If they do not intersect, their radical axis can be constructed **with a straightedge alone** (§ 17) by introducing an auxiliary circle that cuts both, finding the two chords of intersection, and dropping the perpendicular from their meeting point!

For three circles with non-collinear centers, the three radical axes meet at a single unique point: the :term:`radical center <punkt-der-gleichen-potenzen>` (*Punkt der gleichen Potenzen*).
