§ 1: Notes — Why One Circle with Center M Suffices
==================================================

.. rubric:: Historical, Algebraic & Archival Apparatus — Folio 0007 (p. 3)

1. **Why a Straightedge Alone in an Empty Plane Fails**: Any projective collineation maps straight lines to straight lines and preserves incidence and cross-ratios, yet does not preserve distance, parallelism, or right angles—mapping the line at infinity :math:`\ell_\infty` to an arbitrary ordinary line. In an empty plane, the straightedge can construct only projectively invariant configurations (complete quadrangles and harmonic conjugates), not midpoints, parallels, or square roots.
2. **Why One Circle with Center** :math:`M` **Restores the Full Metric**: Center :math:`M` bisects every diameter :math:`ab`, placing the point at infinity as the harmonic conjugate of :math:`M` with respect to :math:`a, b`. By the complete quadrangle (§ 6), this single bisected segment generates all parallels; by Thales' theorem (§ 18, Problem 3), inscribed right angles generate perpendiculars; and by homothety onto :math:`\Omega` (Problem 7), the drawn circumference serves as a permanent quadratic oracle extracting arbitrary square roots.
