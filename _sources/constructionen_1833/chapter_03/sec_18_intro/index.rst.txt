§ 18: Introduction — Reduction of All Constructions to Eight Fundamental Problems
=================================================================================

.. rubric:: Jakob Steiner — *Die geometrischen Constructionen* (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

**Solution of all geometric problems by means of the straightedge, when any single fixed circle is given.**

Having established the harmonic properties of rectilinear figures in Chapter I and the centers of similitude and radical axes of circles in Chapter II, we now fulfill the promise of the Introductory Survey (§ 1): solving the **eight fundamental problems** solely with the ungraduated straightedge in the presence of a single fixed auxiliary circle :math:`\Omega` with given center :math:`M`.

Every Euclidean ruler-and-compass construction reduces to a finite sequence of three elementary intersection operations:

1. **Line–Line Meet**: Directly traced with the straightedge.
2. **Line–Circle Meet**: Solved for any un-drawn circle :math:`(M_1, r_1)` by **Problem 7** via homothetic projection onto :math:`\Omega`.
3. **Circle–Circle Meet**: Solved for any two un-drawn circles by **Problem 8** via their :term:`line of equal powers <linie-der-gleichen-potenzen>` (reducing directly to Problem 7).

Because **Problems 1–5** furnish parallels, rational segment divisions, perpendiculars, and angle transfers/bisections using :math:`\Omega`, and **Problem 6** transfers segments via similitude centers, all of plane elementary geometry is reduced to pure straightedge incidences.
