§ 1. The Core Principle of Fixed-Circle Straightedge Constructions
------------------------------------------------------------------

All construction problems in elementary Euclidean geometry—traditionally requiring both the ungraduated straightedge and the movable compass (:cite:`euclid-elements`)—can be executed using **only the straightedge**, provided that a single :term:`fixed auxiliary circle <fester-huelfskreis>` :math:`\Omega` together with its center :math:`M` is permanently drawn in the plane.

The entire reduction rests upon solving eight fundamental problems (*Fundamental-Aufgaben*, executed in full in Chapter III):

1. **First Problem**: Through a given point, to draw a straight line parallel to a given straight line.
2. **Second Problem**: Given a bounded segment, to multiply it, divide it into :math:`n` equal parts, or construct a segment having any given rational ratio to it.
3. **Third Problem**: Through a given point, to drop a perpendicular onto a given straight line.
4. **Fourth Problem**: At a given point on a given straight line, to lay off an angle equal to a given angle.
5. **Fifth Problem**: To bisect a given angle, or to multiply it by any integer.
6. **Sixth Problem**: From a given point along a given ray, to lay off a segment equal in length to a given segment.
7. **Seventh Problem**: To find the mutual intersection points of a given straight line and a given (un-drawn, virtual) circle :math:`(M_1, r_1)`.
8. **Eighth Problem**: To find the mutual intersection points of two given (un-drawn, virtual) circles :math:`(M_1, r_1)` and :math:`(M_2, r_2)`.

Once these eight problems are mastered, any composite construction involving line–line, line–circle, or circle–circle intersections reduces strictly to line–line intersections drawn with the straightedge alone!

§ 2. Historical Survey: Mascheroni, Poncelet, Lambert, and Brianchon
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The question of minimal geometric instruments has a distinguished lineage:

* In 1797, :person:`Lorenzo Mascheroni <mascheroni>` proved in *La Geometria del Compasso* (:cite:`mascheroni-1797`, translated into German by :person:`J. P. de Gruson <grueson>`, :cite:`grueson-1825`) that all Euclidean point determinations can be executed with **the compass alone**, dispensing entirely with the straightedge.
* In 1822, :person:`Jean-Victor Poncelet <poncelet>` published the seminal remark in *Traité des propriétés projectives des figures* (:cite:`poncelet-1822`, No. 353 ff.) that if one single circle together with its center is permanently drawn in the plane, all ruler-and-compass constructions can conversely be performed with **the straightedge alone**.
* Earlier, :person:`Johann Heinrich Lambert <lambert>` in *Freie Perspective* (:cite:`lambert-1759`) and :person:`Charles Julien Brianchon <brianchon>` in *Application de la théorie des transversales* (:cite:`brianchon-1817`) had solved particular linear constructions using straightedge alone by invoking perspective and transversal properties.

The present work provides the first systematic, elementary, and complete realization of Poncelet's theorem, organized systematically for higher educational instruction and practical drafting.
