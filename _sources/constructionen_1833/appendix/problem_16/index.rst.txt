Appendix Problem 16: Remaining Common Points & Tangents of Two Conics Sharing 2 Points
======================================================================================

.. rubric:: Jakob Steiner — *Die geometrischen Constructionen* (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

« *When two conic sections have two common (intersection) points given, together with any three further points on each conic, to find their remaining two common points as well as their four common tangents.* »

Let the two shared points be :math:`\mathfrak{R}, \mathfrak{S}`, and three further points be :math:`\mathfrak{A}, \mathfrak{B}, \mathfrak{C}` on :math:`K` and :math:`\mathfrak{A}_1, \mathfrak{B}_1, \mathfrak{C}_1` on :math:`K_1` (pp. 74–75 / ``[103]``). Projecting :math:`\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{R}, \mathfrak{B}\mathfrak{S}, \mathfrak{C}\mathfrak{S}` onto :math:`K_1` locates :math:`\mathfrak{a}_1, \mathfrak{b}_1, \mathfrak{c}_1`, and opposite cross-chords :math:`(\mathfrak{AB}, \mathfrak{a}_1\mathfrak{b}_1)` and :math:`(\mathfrak{AC}, \mathfrak{a}_1\mathfrak{c}_1)` define line :math:`\mathfrak{pq}`—the **conjugate common secant** of :math:`\mathfrak{RS}`—cutting :math:`K` (via Problem 2.a) at the remaining two intersections :math:`\mathfrak{r}, \mathfrak{s}`! Choosing an external point :math:`\mathfrak{P}` on :math:`\mathfrak{RS}` and pairing tangents yields the projection centers :math:`A, I` determining all **four common tangents** via Problem 3.
