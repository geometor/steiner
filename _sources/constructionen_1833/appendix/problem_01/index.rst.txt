Appendix Problem 1: Polygon Circumscribed About One and Inscribed in Another
============================================================================

.. rubric:: Jakob Steiner — *Die geometrischen Constructionen* (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

« *Given any two triangles in a plane, to find a third triangle that is simultaneously circumscribed about the first and inscribed in the second.* »

Let :math:`B, B_1, B_2` be the vertices of the first triangle and :math:`A, A_1, A_2` the extended sides of the second. Starting from three trial points :math:`\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}, \mathfrak{c}` on :math:`A`, trace rays cyclically through :math:`B \to A_1 \to B_1 \to A_2 \to B_2 \to A` to obtain image points :math:`\alpha, \beta, \gamma` on :math:`A`. Projecting the three pairs onto fixed circle :math:`\Omega` from pole :math:`P` gives axis :math:`\mathfrak{pq}`, whose intersections :math:`r, s` with :math:`\Omega` yield up to two self-closing triangles (or none if :math:`\mathfrak{pq}` misses :math:`\Omega`).

*Remark (p. 68)*: The exact same method extends to arbitrary :math:`n`-gons (quadrilaterals, pentagons, etc.).
