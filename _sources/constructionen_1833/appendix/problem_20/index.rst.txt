Appendix Problem 20: n-Gon Inscribed in a Conic Passing Through n Given Points
==============================================================================

.. rubric:: Jakob Steiner — *Die geometrischen Constructionen* (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

« *In a conic section given by any five points or five conditions, to inscribe an :math:`n`-gon that is simultaneously circumscribed about any given :math:`n`-gon (i.e., whose sides pass in a definite order through :math:`n` given points).* »

On pp. 76–77 (``[106]``–``[107]``), Steiner recalls the fame of this problem (the Ottajano–Castillon circle case) and solves it on an arbitrary conic :math:`M_2` with straightedge and fixed circle :math:`M` alone: pick three given points :math:`\mathfrak{a}_2, \mathfrak{b}_2, \mathfrak{c}_2` determining an auxiliary circle :math:`M_1`, find similitude centers :math:`A, I` of :math:`M` and :math:`M_1` plus a projection center :math:`P` between :math:`M_1` and :math:`M_2`, transport the :math:`n` points :math:`N_2 \mapsto N_1 \mapsto N`, solve the inscribed :math:`n`-gon :math:`\mathfrak{N}` in fixed circle :math:`M` by Poncelet's straightedge method, and project back :math:`\mathfrak{N} \mapsto \mathfrak{N}_1 \mapsto \mathfrak{N}_2`!
