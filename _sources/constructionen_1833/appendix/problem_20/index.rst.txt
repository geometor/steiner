Appendix Problem 20: n-Gon Inscribed in a Conic Passing Through n Given Points
==============================================================================

.. rubric:: Jakob Steiner — *Die geometrischen Constructionen* (Berlin, 1833 / Leipzig, 1895)

« *In a conic section given by any five points or five conditions, to inscribe an* :math:`n`-*gon that is simultaneously circumscribed about any given* :math:`n`-*gon (i.e., whose sides pass in a definite order through* :math:`n` *given points).* »

This celebrated problem—first solved for the circle by Castillon and generalized by Ottajano and Poncelet (pp. 76–77, ``[106]``–``[107]``)—is solved on an arbitrary conic :math:`M_2` with the straightedge and fixed circle :math:`M` alone as follows: select three given points :math:`\mathfrak{a}_2, \mathfrak{b}_2, \mathfrak{c}_2` determining an auxiliary circle :math:`M_1`, find the centers of similitude :math:`A, I` of :math:`M` and :math:`M_1` together with a center of projection :math:`P` between :math:`M_1` and :math:`M_2`, transport the :math:`n` points :math:`N_2 \mapsto N_1 \mapsto N`, construct the inscribed :math:`n`-gon :math:`\mathfrak{N}` in the fixed circle :math:`M` by Poncelet's straightedge method, and project back :math:`\mathfrak{N} \mapsto \mathfrak{N}_1 \mapsto \mathfrak{N}_2`.
